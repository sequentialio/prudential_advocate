Pillar 1 · Accessible to everyone

Built to WCAG 2.2 Level AA

The U.S. Department of Justice takes the position that the ADA applies to the websites of businesses open to the public. Law-firm sites are frequent targets of accessibility lawsuits. This site is engineered to the current standard—and it ships with an accessibility statement and a built-in reader-controls panel.

What every visitor gets

  • Full keyboard operation—menus, forms, everything—with a visible focus outline
  • Contrast-checked colors; meaning never carried by color alone
  • Real labels, error summaries, and alt text for screen-reader users
  • “Skip to content” link, logical headings, and landmark regions
  • Respects the visitor’s reduced-motion setting; readable at 200% zoom

The reader-controls panel

Every page has a floating accessibility button. One click lets a visitor turn on high contrast, larger text, underlined links, or reduced motion—and their choice is remembered as they browse. Try it in the panel above, or with the button in the corner of this page.

Pillar 2 · California law-firm rules

Compliant attorney advertising, by design

An attorney’s website is regulated advertising in California. The site is built around the Rules of Professional Conduct (7.1–7.5), Business & Professions Code §§ 6157–6159, the new SB 37 (effective January 1, 2026), and CalOPPA.

No misleading communications (Rule 7.1)

The old site showed one attorney’s bar admissions and degrees on every staff page—including paralegals and the intern. That could be read as misrepresenting qualifications. The new site is role-aware: only attorneys have a credentials panel, and only their own. The single worst liability in the old site is fixed.

Required disclosures present (Rule 7.2 & SB 37)

The footer of every page names the responsible attorney (Robert W. J. Heaven, Esq.) and a physical office address, and carries an “attorney advertising” notice—exactly what SB 37 now expects of law-firm sites.

No guarantees, no “specialist” claims

Copy avoids guarantees of success, superiority claims, and “specialist/expert/certified” language. We use safe phrasing—“focuses on,” “practices in”—and a clear “past results don’t guarantee future outcomes” line.

Disclaimers & privacy (CalOPPA)

New Disclaimer, Privacy Policy, and Accessibility pages—none existed before. The contact form states that submitting it does not create an attorney-client relationship and warns against sending confidential details.

These compliance features are drafting guidance built into the site, not legal advice. The disclaimer, privacy policy, and all advertising copy are ready for your review and sign-off before launch.
